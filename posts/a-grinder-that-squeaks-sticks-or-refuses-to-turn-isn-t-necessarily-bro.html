A grinder that squeaks, sticks, or refuses to turn isn’t necessarily broken.

This post covers why grinders lock up and how to avoid making the situation worse. The typical offender is a thin film of compressed plant material and resin. That film binds the teeth, the threads, or the screen together until the parts stop moving.

That buildup occurs because resin is tacky and plant matter compacts under pressure. Over time the bond increases where the teeth meet the lid, along the threads, and across the screen. The resistance points out where to clean, not where to apply force.

Forcing the parts risks the coating. Scratch the finish and the grinder will look worse and attract more resin later. You also run the risk of bending the lid so it no longer seats correctly on the threads.

And there’s the kief problem — send precious kief flying across the counter; I learned that lesson the messy way. Kief is lightweight and often flakes loose when a jam finally gives.

Instead of forcing, work the jam gently. Use a soft brush or a wooden pick to clear the teeth and tease compressed bits from the threads. Tap the chamber over a tray to catch loose kief.

If metal parts are removable, separate them and clean each piece. A small wire or nylon brush cleans screens without tearing them. Avoid hard metal scrapers that can scratch the finish.

Rinse or soak parts if the material allows, then dry completely before reassembly to prevent sticking from moisture. Keep the screen free of clogged holes so airflow and kief collection stay reliable.

Make cleaning a routine before a jam starts. A quick brush after each session prevents that thin layer from ever gluing the grinder shut.
